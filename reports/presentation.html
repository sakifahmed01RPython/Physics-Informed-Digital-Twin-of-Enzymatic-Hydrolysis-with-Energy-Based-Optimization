01 — Data rescue: 40 rows → 31 usable

The raw tables were hostile: Excel time-format cells, impossible DH values (up to 97.7%), non-enzymatic rows, and dry-basis composition rows in a wet-basis file. The audit removed 9 rows with a written trail (data/audit_report.md) and kept 0 NaNs. E/S was locked to a constant 0.02 — that fact drove the dose-sweep arm of the campaign.

02 — The physics core: ODE twin calibrated

The mixed-order hydrolysis ODE (S → P with competitive product inhibition, Arrhenius temperature dependence, Gaussian pH activity, plus a substrate-heads activation term) was fitted to all five Alcalase time courses. 4 of 5 curves land inside the ±3 DH-pt tolerance; BATCH_011 (RMSE 4.19) is flagged rather than silently trusted.

03 — Bioactivity: ORAC ridge baseline

A composition+process ridge predicts antioxidant capacity in leave-one-out (R² = 0.507, MAE 28.3). It is a ranking tool, not a prophecy — the composite DH+ORAC objective is the next step, not this one.

04 — Active learning: energy-based acquisition

The EBM learns an energy landscape over (process × composition × enzyme). Expected improvement is computed by interior-anchored Langevin sampling over the energy. The star markers are the three acquired experiments — physically sensible, high-EI points (e.g. E/S doses on Alcalase).

05 — The campaign: 15 runs, 3 arms

gap_fill (7) brings rare enzymes to n≥3 so the EBM may acquire over them; process_lhc (5) explores the Alcalase process box and new materials; dose_sweep (3) restores the E/S identifiability that the constant-0.02 history destroyed. Per-batch lab sheets and a runbook were generated from the design.

06 — The loop so far

Two acquisition rounds are logged (3 proposals each). Best observed DH is pinned at the historical 50.0% — it will move only when campaign measurements land. Each batch enters via a validated template, then the loop retrains on all rows.
